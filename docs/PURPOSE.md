# Purpose Statement

## What this system is

**WMS Linker** is an operations platform that connects online commerce (for example Shopify) with warehouse and fulfillment systems. It sits between the storefront and the warehouse so orders, inventory decisions, shipments, and returns stay in sync without teams juggling multiple portals for every step.

Its job is simple to state and hard to do well:

> Take a customer order from the channel where it was sold, decide where it should be fulfilled, hand it to the right warehouse or 3PL, bring tracking and status back, update the store, and keep a clear record of what happened—including returns.

---

## Purpose

We exist to make **fulfillment visible, controllable, and trustworthy** for companies that sell online and ship from one or more warehouses.

Operators should be able to answer, from one place:

- Where is this order right now?
- Why was it routed to this warehouse?
- Has the warehouse received it? Has it shipped?
- Is Shopify (or the sales channel) updated?
- What went wrong, and what do we do next?
- How do returns and restocks close the loop?

The system is built so people do not need to leave the portal to check the store admin, a carrier site, and a warehouse tool for the same order.

---

## What makes it a good system

### 1. One spine for the whole order lifecycle

Orders move through a clear path: **receive → route → allocate → fulfill → ship → sync → return (when needed)**. That spine is the same whether you use modern WMS integrations or classic EDI/SFTP handoffs. Consistency reduces training time and mistakes.

### 2. Routing with reasons, not guesswork

Warehouse choice is driven by rules and inventory—not ad‑hoc tribal knowledge. When an order is assigned, the system can show *why* (proximity, stock, split shipment, SLA, and so on). That makes operations auditable and easier to improve.

### 3. Channel and warehouse stay in sync

Fulfillment in the warehouse is not finished until the sales channel reflects it. The linker tracks shipment state and channel sync status so “shipped in WMS but still open on the store” is visible and fixable, not silent.

### 4. Works with how warehouses already work

Not every site runs the same stack. The platform supports modern WMS workflows and legacy EDI/SFTP patterns so companies can adopt stronger tooling without throwing away existing 3PL processes overnight.

### 5. Multi-warehouse and split shipments without chaos

When stock lives in more than one place, the system can split an order into fulfillment groups, track each branch, and still present a single order journey to the operator. That keeps complex reality usable.

### 6. Returns are part of the product, not an afterthought

Returns, dispositions, and restock paths are first-class. Closing the loop on inventory after a return matters as much as getting the outbound shipment right.

### 7. Operators see the journey, not only tables

Order detail is designed as an **operations cockpit**: progress, connected systems, package journey, customer and line facts, returns, actions, and activity in one screen. Less context-switching means fewer errors and faster resolution.

### 8. Clear ownership and roles

Platform, company, and warehouse/uploader roles keep responsibilities separated: who configures shops and rules, who runs day-to-day fulfillment, who uploads shipping confirmations. Good systems encode process, not just data.

### 9. Traceability by default

Activity logs, file links (such as EDI 940), tracking IDs, and sync states create a trail you can defend with customers, finance, and warehouse partners. When something fails, the record shows *where* it failed.

### 10. Built to grow with the business

Start with a single warehouse and a single shop. Add warehouses, routing rules, carriers, and integrations as volume grows—without rewriting how “an order” works.

## Guiding principles

1. **Truth lives in the order** — status, warehouse, tracking, and sync should be readable from the order itself.  
2. **Automate the happy path; surface the exceptions** — routing and sync should run quietly; failures must be loud and actionable.  
3. **Prefer clarity over cleverness** — operators should trust what they see without decoding jargon.  
4. **Respect warehouse reality** — support both modern APIs and established EDI/SFTP habits.  
5. **Close every loop** — outbound, channel update, and returns/restock should all complete or clearly stall with a reason.

## In one sentence

**WMS Linker is the control layer between commerce and the warehouse—so every order has a clear path, every handoff is visible, and fulfillment stays accurate from click to doorstep (and back again when needed).**
